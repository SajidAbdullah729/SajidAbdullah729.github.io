About me

I am a software engineer who builds production RAG systems, agentic workflows, and scalable backends meant to ship - not just demos. My day-to-day work centers on retrieval quality, multimodal grounding, orchestration of AI agents, and the infrastructure around them: async APIs, caching, message queues, observability, and deployment patterns that keep latency and cost under control.

That systems focus sits on a strong competitive programming foundation. I am an ACM ICPC Asia-West Continent finalist and an Expert-rated Codeforces competitor. I have competed in ICPC regionals and continent finals, NCPC, and IUPC events representing my university, and I have also participated in Meta Hacker Cup, qualifying for Round 2. The same habits that win contests - clear problem decomposition, careful edge-case thinking, and disciplined implementation - shape how I design services and debug production issues.

In research, I worked on recognizing doctors’ handwritten prescriptions in Bangladesh - where most prescriptions are still handwritten and illegibility can lead to medication errors. We built a pioneering local dataset of pharmaceutical word images and applied a VGG16 deep neural network to digitize medical handwriting. The work was published at IEEE iCACCESS 2024 and released the dataset publicly, with the goal of improving prescription clarity, patient safety, and safer healthcare digitization in developing settings.

Beyond competing, I give back to the programming community through judging, organizing, and contest testing. That includes serving as a problem tester for Codeforces Round #957 (Div. 3) on Codeforces - one of the world’s largest competitive programming platforms, with a global audience of hundreds of thousands of contestants. My role was to stress-test statements, samples, and edge cases before release so the round stayed fair, clear, and technically sound for that international community.

Across machine learning research and full-stack delivery, I aim for reliable engineering: readable systems, measurable quality, and solutions that hold up under real load.

Research

Peer-reviewed work on deep learning for prescription digitization in Bangladesh.

2024

A Deep Neural Network Approach with Pioneering Local Dataset to Recognize Doctor's Handwritten Prescription in Bangladesh

IEEE iCACCESS 2024 · AUST

Problem. Most Bangladeshi doctors still write prescriptions by hand (~97%), and poor legibility raises medication-error risk.

Approach. Applied a VGG16 deep neural network to recognize and digitize medical handwriting from local prescriptions.

Dataset. Built a pioneering Bangladeshi dataset of pharmaceutical word images (4,680 samples across 78 medicine classes), collected and screened from hospital prescriptions.

Impact. Aimed to improve prescription clarity and patient safety, and to support safer healthcare digitization in developing settings.

IEEE Xplore · DOI · Dataset on Kaggle
